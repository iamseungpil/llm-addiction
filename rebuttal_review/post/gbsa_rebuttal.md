**[W1]** Accepted: the title invites a literal reading we never test. Camera-ready it becomes *Autonomy and Gambling-Like Risk-Taking in Large Language Models*, with distortion names labelled instruments, not diagnoses.

**[W2]** Your suspicion is partly correct. In a persona × rationality × betting-mode factorial (cap $70, n = 100/cell), the persona alone lifts Gemini's forced-arm participation from 8/100 to 53/100 and its ruin from 0 to 12, and its choosing-arm ruin from 4 to 32; we withdraw "stable risk preferences".

We separate the two models rather than generalise from one. In LLaMA's choosing arm the persona changes nothing — ruin 81.5% [75.5, 86.3] without it (n = 200) against 82.0% [73.3, 88.3] with it (n = 100) — so where the policy is already engaged the persona adds no risk on top. Gemini is the harder case: its choosing-arm participation is 100/100 either way, yet rounds (2.25 to 8.43) and ruin (4 to 32 of 100) still move, so in that model the persona does more than remove refusal, and we will not claim otherwise. There was also little refusal to remove: recomputed with the phrase list committed and Claude excluded for truncated replies, safety-style declining appears in 0–13% of persona-free first-round stops (0/99, 0/99, 12/92), no contrast reaching p < 0.05. The factorial enters the camera-ready.

**[W3]** Accepted for the figures, which will carry 95% Wilson intervals with their n. Here every cell carries its n, the Q1 and Q3 ruin rates carry Wilson intervals, and the W4 sweep is a direction across cells where no single cell separates at n = 50. The appendix does carry bootstrap intervals excluding zero on the variable-minus-fixed gap (Gemma +0.054 [+0.043, +0.066]): the uncertainty was computed, the figures did not show it.

**[W4]** Partly accepted; the newest result sits here. Choosing a stake changes more than freedom — action space, game length, stopping — and game length is the operative part, which equal caps do not touch. So we ran the test that does: on wagering games only, how much ruin has each arm produced by the time it has staked a given total? Swept from $100 upward (below that ruin is impossible), the choosing arm ruins more in all four cells at all six thresholds, 24 of 24. At the tightest threshold, forced versus choosing as a share of wagering games: Gemini base 10.3 vs 14.0, Gemini with all five modules 12.0 vs 26.0, GPT-4.1-mini 3.1 vs 8.0, GPT-4o-mini 0.0 vs 6.0 (29, 50, 32, 18 wagering games). No cell alone is significant at n = 50; the direction across cells carries the claim, and the sweep is post-hoc.

We think equal total stake is a conservative estimand, not a fairness correction: that total is partly the mechanism, since betting small buys more rounds and rounds produce ruin. We also accept a sharper name: without discretion the control is a **forced-maximum arm**, since at a forced $70 stake most models decline to play at all. Camera-ready: both estimands side by side, the arm renamed.

**[Q1]** No longer one. Our grid used the base prompt, where four of six models never ruin in either arm, leaving LLaMA the only informative case (forced 3.0% [1.4, 6.4] against choosing 81.5% [75.5, 86.3], n = 200). The cells below are separate ones: they carry the persona preamble in both arms and run at n = 50, so absolute rates are not comparable across the two, though the forced-versus-choosing contrast within each is valid because the persona is held constant. Under the paper's full five-module condition at cap $70, forced versus choosing bankruptcy: Gemini 20.0 [11.2, 33.0] vs 62.0 [48.2, 74.1], GPT-4.1-mini 2.0 [0.4, 10.5] vs 56.0 [42.3, 68.8], GPT-4o-mini 0.0 [0.0, 7.1] vs 40.0 [27.6, 53.8]; Gemini shows it under the base prompt too, 6.0 [2.1, 16.2] vs 34.0 [22.4, 47.8]. With LLaMA, four of six. The cap-$70 block is complete, 16 of 16 cells, and 51 of 64 grid cells now pass our data-quality guard; we report by 3 August.

**[Q2]** Lists and rules yes, validation not yet. **Lists** (representative patterns): *pattern belief* — `pattern`, `hidden`, `machine.{0,20}(hot|cold|due)`; *loss chasing* — `recover`, `win back`, `recoup`; *probability misestimation* — `due for`, `overdue`, `bound to win`; *goal escalation* — `(new|revised|updated).{0,10}(target|goal)`. **Rule**: one trace, one construct, one question ("does this response use [construct] as grounds for its next action?"), four levels — grounds / mentions but rejects / unrelated / cannot tell. **Validation**: 198 blinded items (4 constructs × 25 × 2 arms, minus 2 duplicate traces); coders see only trace and construct name. Those four are the frozen convergent codebook's — illusion of control, gambler's fallacy, impaired control, self-serving bias — the first three mapping onto pattern belief, probability misestimation and loss chasing. It does not cover goal escalation, which has no counterpart there and carries the largest share of the original goal contrast. Thresholds were fixed before any label: κ below 0.60 silences quantitative claims, per-frame precision below 0.50 drops a frame from the body, a contrast interval covering zero withdraws the claim. Interim state: items drawn and audited, zero labels; we report 3 August.

**[Q3]** Your reading — that continuing may just mean "I am supposed to play" — was precise enough to test twice. First, the paper's own data: two of the five prompt modules state the payout (3×) and the win rate (30%), so −$0.10 per dollar follows by arithmetic. Across the 32 conditions, the choosing-arm cells where expected value is computable ruin more, not less, in all four models with stored text (GPT-4o-mini 18.8% [15.2, 22.9] against 2.2% [1.5, 3.2], n = 400 and 1,200 games; at matched module counts the gap survives at two and three, vanishing at four). Giving the model the odds does not reduce ruin.

Having the inputs is not doing the arithmetic, so we ran the stronger manipulation you named: an instruction giving the per-round expected loss, stating that stopping now is EV-optimal, and permitting a stop at any time (cap $70, n = 100/cell). Without a persona it cuts LLaMA's choosing-arm ruin from 81.5% [75.5, 86.3] to 3.0% [1.0, 8.5], a real reduction that supports part of your account. Two things survive it. It is no off-switch: 69 of 100 games still contain a wager, and the model survives by wagering small — the discretion mechanism, not its absence. And with a persona it removes nothing: forced-arm ruin is 6.0% [2.8, 12.5] without the instruction against 7.0% [3.4, 13.7] with it. 42 of 44 cells are collected; we report 3 August.

**[Q4]** We ran this additional experiment, the result supports your point, and we will update the paper. You named the control our design lacked, so we built it: a nested cross-validated R-squared test on the published cell (Gemma, slot machine, choosing arm, layer 22, n = 12,246 decisions) against 65 observable game-log covariates. A reproduction check confirms we recover that cell, R-squared 0.16736 against the published 0.167. We build the cross-validation folds two ways, by game as the paper does and by game-state hash, since 39.3% of rows repeat a state from another game. The extra R-squared each internal block buys over the game log, against a 0.017 threshold fixed before we ran it: raw hidden state +0.059 under both fold rules; SAE features +0.044 by game but **+0.0024 by state, indistinguishable from zero**, only 29.5% of resamples clearing it. The paper's own SAE block is the one that does not survive. In the paper's own residual metric the game log alone recovers 84% of the published cell (0.1400 of 0.1674) and those features add +0.037 to +0.045 on top, which does survive state grouping.

One disclosure on the logit half: our registered baseline named game state, choice probability and logit features, and we dropped the latter two — a deviation from a rule marked fixed in advance. Conditioning on the model's own choice probability when the target is that decision would rig the test, so only the game-state baseline was re-run.

We chose SAE features plus ridge because they are inspectable, letting us steer and remove specific directions; they move behaviour for a direction defined from the model's own betting (z = +4.45 against a twenty-direction null) but not for the direction fitted to predict betting — exactly the redundancy your baseline tests. Camera-ready: the nested test enters the neural section, and the SAE decoder becomes a monitoring signal, not one shown to add over game state.

**[Limitations]** Adopted in your terms and moved into the contributions: these are artificial negative-expected-value games that cannot stand in for real financial, planning or tool-using agents, and the internal results are exploratory monitoring signals, not a mechanism.
