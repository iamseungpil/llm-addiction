# Response to Reviewer KuK5

Both weaknesses named real gaps. We ran new experiments for each; two go against us.

- **W1 — can the neural decoding support a mechanistic reading?** No; the submitted abstract already says the analysis "does not claim circuit-level mechanism". But the null is direction-specific: the direction *fitted to predict* betting does not move behaviour; one *defined from* the model's own betting does.
- **W2 — does the equal-cap claim rest on one model?** It did. Both arms now run at the same cap across the panel under two prompt conditions and in a second harness; under the paper's own condition set the dissociation appears in four of six models.
- **Q1 — does it hold on the other five?** Four of six. Gemma is 0.0% in both arms at all four caps, Claude 0.0 in both arms of the panel — nulls, not absences.
- **Q2 — a positive result, and a balance/round correlate?** Four criteria, below, applied to both directions. We also steered the balance/round direction itself and ran the nested test your question implies; **it supports your point.**

## [W2, Q1] The equal-cap test across the panel

**Our first extension was uninformative.** The published ablation crosses each cap with 32 prompt conditions × 50 repetitions (n = 1,300–1,600): the fixed-bet (forced) arm 0.0/4.7/0.4% against the variable-bet (choosing) arm 14.3/16.4/17.3% at $30/$50/$70, matching the Figure 3d caption. Our extension collapsed that to the base prompt (six models × four caps × both arms, n = 200, 48 of 48 cells), where three of the five non-quarantined models never go bankrupt in either arm. That is the condition, not drift: the paper's own corpus restricted to BASE has the same floor (0/50 in four models, 1/50 in GPT-4o-mini), and Gemma is frozen. Claude's cells there are quarantined: a token-limit defect truncated its replies before the verdict; the panel below uses re-collected ones.

LLaMA is decisive there: at cap $70 the forced arm stakes $68.4 per round and ruins in 3.0% of games, the choosing arm $32.1 and 81.5%. The arm offered the larger stake survives — range expansion cannot produce that. Exposure differs: 0.92 rounds per game forced against 15.17 choosing.

**Both pre-registered rules failed, and we say so.** The primary — lower 2.5% posterior quantile of the arm effect > 0 — is ill-posed: the forced arm is 0 in every cell, so no data this design produces could reject it; our output's pass came from an unregistered pooled interval, which we withdraw. The secondary is 0 of 6: given the choice, models wager 22–46% of the cap, not the registered half.

**The paper's condition set recovers the effect.** Separate cells: four API models, all five modules (G/M/H/W/P), cap $70, n = 50. Both arms carry the same task preamble, which the published ablation did not, so absolute rates are not comparable to the grid. Ruin, forced against choosing: Gemini 20.0 vs 62.0%, GPT-4.1-mini 2.0 vs 56.0, GPT-4o-mini 0.0 vs 40.0, Claude 0.0 vs 0.0. Gemini with that preamble but *without* the five modules moves the same way, 6.0 vs 34.0, so it is not the cause. With LLaMA, four of six.

**The cleanest cell.** Gemini with five modules is the only one where both arms play all 50 games, so refusal cannot explain the gap; there the forced arm stakes more per round ($64.5 vs $47.9) and ruins less (Fisher p = 3.6e-05). Equal caps equalise the per-round maximum, not the total staked; at matched cumulative stake (six thresholds) the choosing arm still ruins more in every cell at every threshold — 24 of 24, smallest gap +3.7 points; post-hoc, intervals overlap at n = 50.

**A second, independently pre-registered factorial repeats it at that cap.** Gemini, no rationality instruction, n = 100 per cell: the forced arm bankrupts 12 games at 1.06 rounds per game, the choosing arm 32 at 8.43. LLaMA in that same cell: forced 6.0% against choosing 82.0%. 43 of 44 cells are complete; the eight Claude cells have been re-collected at 100% decision completeness, zero API fallbacks; the last open-weight cell reports by 3 August.

**Status.** Claude 3.5 Haiku is end-of-life (404), so the panels substitute Claude Haiku 4.5, a different model. They sit in a 64-cell matched-cap ladder; 59 clear our guard of 95% readable decisions, including all 16 cap-$70 cells, the source of every panel rate above. The rest report by 3 August.

## [W1, Q2] What the internal-state results show

**Why the causal result came out weak: where we wrote, not whether the activations matter.** All three published protocols acted at layer 22, and the steering arm acted on the last prompt token alone, at n = 50 per dose, along the direction fitted to predict betting. The submitted Limitations already left the locus open — the effect is "not consolidated at L22 in a form that single-layer patching can write into the model", with an "earlier-layer, distributed multi-layer pathway, or SAE-feature subspace target" named as the open question. We have now run the multi-layer arm.

**What we ran.** *First*, a width-6 window scan tiling every layer of both models, to locate where writing works at all: single layers are insufficient, Gemma localises to L16–21, and on LLaMA four candidate windows were steered head-to-head with L14–19 winning. *Second*, inside that band we steer and remove frozen unit axes on the raw residual stream — at every token position on prefill and on every decode step, 200 games per dose cell, seeds tied to the trial index so doses are paired by construction. *Third*, the same battery on the balance/round confound direction and on twenty norm-matched random directions, so the comparison is against edits of equal total size.

**What came out.** A *behaviourally defined* axis — the difference between activations on high-bet and low-bet decisions, with no probe fitted — moves Gemma monotonically across all seven doses (α = −3 to +3): 0.009, 0.182 unperturbed, 0.286. Slope 0.0457, z = +4.45 against the twenty matched directions; removal lowers betting by 0.037 on Gemma and 0.052 on LLaMA, 200 seed-matched pairs each. The slope tracks the prompt condition (+G 0.0469 against +M 0.0218, difference 0.0237, 95% interval [+0.018, +0.030]), its sign is task-specific (LLaMA betting +0.142, investment-choice risk −0.20, mystery-wheel spinning −0.36), and it survives with no autoencoder at all (raw-ridge 0.0284, z ≈ +3) — so it is neither a uniform push nor an artefact of the sparse basis. Self-limit: the LLaMA removal passes by a 6% margin, and its post-removal projections came from a layer outside the located window.

**The direction you ask about stays null after the repair**: steering z = +0.75 against the same null, removal p = .885 (Gemma) and p = 1.0 (LLaMA). Under one alternative specification it does move (+0.086 at α = +2, p < 1e−4), but parse success collapses from 0.80 to 0.34 against our 0.45 gate (n = 50, exploratory, not replicated), so we do not count it. The prompt-swap arm was not re-run. **The readout therefore stays a monitoring signal, and we claim no identity between it and the behavioural axis.**

**What a positive result would have looked like (Q2).** For steering: (1) up raises the betting index and down lowers it, interval on the difference excluding zero; (2) the effect grows across the ladder, not only at the extreme dose; (3) it clears a band of matched random directions; (4) parse success does not degrade with dose. The fitted direction meets (2); meets (3) at one dose in one direction; is untested on (1), which we do not count in our favour; and fails (4) in the one specification where it moves. The behavioural axis meets (2) and (3), removal supplying the down half of (1); flat or inside the random band, we would have called it null too. For patching, dose becomes donor state; for prompt swap, the projection must keep tracking behaviour.

**Your residualisation question.** Balance and round are already removed in the published number: the appendix caption on the 0.167 cell states, in the version you read, that they are statistically removed before Ridge with 5-fold GroupKFold by game id, and the layer-sweep caption adds that the deconfound runs **within fold**. We ran the same battery on the confound direction itself: it steers at chance (z = +0.64), removal leaves LLaMA unchanged (p = 1.0) and moves Gemma the wrong way (+0.046), and its cosine with the raw-ridge betting axis is 0.000.

But that is not a nested comparison against a rich observable baseline, and it leaves the betting history in — above all the previous wager. **We ran it, and the result supports your point.** On the published cell's own 12,246 decisions across 1,596 games we built a 65-covariate game-log baseline (drawdown, streaks, cumulative stake, lagged bet ratios). The reproduction gate passes at 0.16736 against the published 0.167. But 4,808 rows (39.3%) duplicate a state from a *different* game, so game-id grouping cannot keep a state off both sides of a split. Grouping by state hash instead, the published cell survives (0.16736 → 0.16095), and on the clipped bet-ratio target the raw hidden state raises held-out $R^2$ from +0.588 to +0.647 (+0.0591; 99.5% of 200 resamples clear our pre-set 0.017 margin) — richer recovery of the same policy, we think, not information the log lacks. **The features the paper fits its direction from do not reach that bar: +0.0024, indistinguishable from zero** (median −0.034, 29.5% clearing). On the deconfounded residual, the metric the published 0.167 is in, the game log alone reaches 0.140 — 84% of the cell — and the SAE adds +0.037 to +0.045 on top, which does survive state grouping.
