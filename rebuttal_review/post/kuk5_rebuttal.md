# Response to Reviewer KuK5

We thank the reviewer for reading our paper so closely, and for the chance to improve it. Both weaknesses named real gaps. We ran new experiments for each; two go against us.

- **W1 — can the neural decoding support a mechanistic reading?** No; the submitted abstract already says the analysis "does not claim circuit-level mechanism". But the null is direction-specific: the direction *fitted to predict* betting does not move behaviour; one *defined from* the model's betting does, across tasks, with the signs fixed beforehand.
- **W2 — does the equal-cap claim rest on one model?** It did. Both arms now run at the same cap across the panel, under two prompt conditions and in a second harness; under the paper's own condition set the dissociation appears in four of six.
- **Q1 — does it hold on the other five?** Four of six. Gemma is 0.0% in both arms at all four caps, Claude 0.0 in both panel arms — nulls, not absences.
- **Q2 — a positive result, and a balance/round correlate?** Four criteria below, applied to both directions. We also steered the balance/round direction itself and ran the nested test your question implies; **it supports your point.**

## [W2, Q1] The equal-cap test across the panel

**Our first extension was uninformative.** The published ablation crosses each cap with 32 prompt conditions × 50 repetitions (n = 1,300–1,600): forced 0.0/4.7/0.4% against choosing 14.3/16.4/17.3% at $30/$50/$70, matching the Figure 3d caption. Our extension used the base prompt only (six models × four caps × both arms, n = 200, 48 of 48 cells), where three of the five non-quarantined models never go bankrupt in either arm. That is the condition, not drift: the paper's corpus restricted to BASE has the same floor, and Gemma is frozen. Claude's cells there are quarantined — a token-limit defect truncated its replies — and the panel below uses re-collected ones.

LLaMA is decisive there: at cap $70 the forced arm stakes $68.4 per round and ruins in 3.0% of games, the choosing arm $32.1 and 81.5%. The arm offered the larger stake survives, which a larger per-round stake cannot produce. Exposure is the other half: 0.92 rounds per game against 15.17.

**Both pre-registered rules failed, and we say so.** The primary — lower 2.5% posterior quantile of the arm effect > 0 — is ill-posed: with the forced arm at 0 in every cell the rule collapses into asking whether the choosing arm ever ruins, which is not the hypothesis it was registered to test. Our output's pass came from an unregistered pooled interval, which we withdraw. The secondary is 0 of 6: given the choice, models wager 22–46% of the cap, not the registered half.

**The paper's condition set recovers the effect.** Separate cells: four API models, all five modules (G/M/H/W/P), cap $70, n = 50. Both arms carry the same task preamble, which the published ablation did not, so absolute rates are not comparable to the grid. Ruin, forced against choosing: Gemini 20.0 vs 62.0%, GPT-4.1-mini 2.0 vs 56.0, GPT-4o-mini 0.0 vs 40.0, Claude 0.0 vs 0.0. Gemini with that preamble but *without* the five modules moves the same way, 6.0 vs 34.0, so it is not the cause. With LLaMA, four of six.

**The cleanest cell.** Gemini with five modules is the only one where both arms play all 50 games, so refusal cannot explain the gap; there the forced arm stakes more per round ($64.5 vs $47.9) and ruins less (Fisher p = 3.6e-05). Equal caps equalise the per-round maximum, not the total staked; at matched cumulative stake the choosing arm still ruins more in every cell at every threshold — 24 of 24, post-hoc, intervals overlapping at n = 50.

**A second pre-registered factorial repeats it at that cap.** Gemini, no rationality instruction, n = 100: forced bankrupts 12 games, choosing 32. LLaMA there: forced 6.0% against choosing 82.0%. All 44 cells are complete.

**Status.** Claude 3.5 Haiku is end-of-life (404), so the panels substitute Claude Haiku 4.5, a different model. Of the 64-cell ladder, 60 clear our guard of 95% readable decisions, including all 16 cap-$70 cells, where every panel rate above comes from. The rest report by 3 August.

## [W1, Q2] What the internal-state results show

**Why the causal result came out weak: where we wrote, not whether the activations matter.** All three published protocols acted at layer 22, and the steering arm on the last prompt token alone, at n = 50 per dose, along the direction fitted to predict betting. The submitted Limitations already left the locus open — the effect is "not consolidated at L22 in a form that single-layer patching can write into the model", with an "earlier-layer, distributed multi-layer pathway, or SAE-feature subspace target" as the open question. We have now run the multi-layer arm.

**What we ran.** *First*, a width-6 window scan tiling every layer of both models, to locate where writing works at all: single layers are insufficient, Gemma localises to L16–21, and on LLaMA four candidate windows were steered head-to-head, L14–19 winning. *Second*, inside that band we steer and remove frozen unit axes on the raw residual stream, at every token position on prefill and every decode step, 200 games per dose cell, seeds tied to the trial index so doses are paired by construction. *Third*, the same battery on the balance/round confound direction and on twenty norm-matched random directions, so edits of equal size are compared.

**What came out.** A *behaviourally defined* axis — the difference between activations on high-bet and low-bet decisions, no probe fitted — moves Gemma monotonically across all seven doses (α = −3 to +3): 0.009, 0.182 unperturbed, 0.286. Slope 0.0457, z = +4.45 against the twenty matched directions; removal lowers betting by 0.037 on Gemma and 0.052 on LLaMA, 200 seed-matched pairs each. The slope tracks the prompt condition (+G 0.0469 against +M 0.0218, difference 0.0237, 95% interval [+0.018, +0.030]), and survives with no autoencoder at all (raw-ridge 0.0284, z ≈ +3), so it is not an artefact of the sparse basis.

**And it transfers across tasks with every sign fixed beforehand.** The three tasks' behavioural axes align where the endpoint directions did not (pairwise cosine +0.43 to +0.67 against at most 0.04). The loadings of the direction they most agree on fixed the predicted sign of every steering cell *before any trial ran*. On Gemma the shared axis then lowers slot-machine betting (z = −3.7) and raises investment-choice risk (z = +3.5): 7 of 10 pre-registered cells. The three misses are mystery-wheel cells at that task's ceiling, and the full matrix is clean only on Gemma. Reading does not cross tasks; writing does. Self-limit: the LLaMA removal passes by a 6% margin, and its post-removal projections came from a layer outside the located window.

**The direction you ask about stays null after the repair**: steering z = +0.75 against the same null, removal p = .885 (Gemma) and 1.0 (LLaMA). Under one alternative specification it does move (+0.086 at α = +2, p < 1e−4), but parse success collapses from 0.80 to 0.34 against our 0.45 gate (n = 50, exploratory), so we do not count it. The prompt-swap arm was not re-run. **The readout therefore stays a monitoring signal, and we claim no identity between it and the behavioural axis.**

**What a positive result would have looked like (Q2).** For steering: (1) up raises the betting index and down lowers it, interval on the difference excluding zero; (2) the effect grows across the ladder, not only at the extreme dose; (3) it clears a band of matched random directions; (4) parse success does not degrade with dose. The fitted direction meets (2); meets (3) at one dose in one direction; is untested on (1), which we do not count in our favour; and fails (4) where it moves. The behavioural axis meets (2) and (3), removal supplying the down half of (1).

**Your residualisation question.** Balance and round are already removed in the published number: the appendix caption on the 0.167 cell states, in the version you read, that they are removed before Ridge under 5-fold GroupKFold by game id, and the layer-sweep caption adds that the deconfound runs **within fold**. We ran the same battery on the confound direction itself: it steers at chance (z = +0.64), removal leaves LLaMA unchanged (p = 1.0) and moves Gemma the wrong way, and its cosine with the raw-ridge betting axis is 0.000.

But that is not a nested comparison against a rich observable baseline, and it leaves the betting history in — above all the previous wager. **We ran it, and the result supports your point.** On the published cell's own 12,246 decisions across 1,596 games we built a 65-covariate game-log baseline (drawdown, streaks, cumulative stake, lagged bet ratios); the reproduction gate passes at 0.16736 against 0.167. But 4,808 rows (39.3%) duplicate a state from a *different* game, so game-id grouping cannot keep a state off both sides of a split. Grouping by state hash instead, the published cell survives (0.16736 → 0.16095), and on the clipped bet-ratio target the raw hidden state raises held-out $R^2$ from +0.588 to +0.647 (99.5% of 200 resamples clear our pre-set 0.017 margin) — richer recovery of the same policy, we think, not information the log lacks. **The features the paper fits its direction from do not reach that bar: +0.0024, indistinguishable from zero** (median −0.034, 29.5% clearing). On the deconfounded residual, the metric the published 0.167 is in, the game log alone reaches 0.140 — 84% of the cell — and the SAE adds +0.037 on top, which does survive state grouping.
