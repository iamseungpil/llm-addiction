# Response to the Area Chair

Thank you for the summary. It names the right fault line: do these concerns need studies a rebuttal cannot contain? For two of the three we think not, and we ran the experiments that decide them.

| experiment run for this response | what it decides | result |
|---|---|---|
| equal-cap grid, five models | does the fixed-variable dissociation hold beyond one model? | the variable arm ruins at least as often in 29 of 32 pairs; 9 of the 12 API differences exclude zero, none negative |
| policy-choice experiment, four arms | is the risk induced by autonomy, or by something narrower? | a self-chosen stake behaves like a forced one, +3.0 pp; only per-round discretion raises ruin, +80.0 pp; widening the range does nothing, −5.0 pp |
| second coding instrument, definitions-derived and frozen | is the distortion result an artefact of our own lexicon? | the contrast reproduces in 6 of 6 models, every interval excluding zero |
| behavioural-axis intervention and removal, two models | can anything in the activation space be shown to change betting? | betting rises with dose and falls when the component is removed, in both models; the submitted readout stays inert |
| demonstration experiment, 16 API cells | does one worked example move behaviour? | in the one cell with room, a cautious example cuts ruin to 21% and an escalating one raises it to 52% |

**Framing.** The title becomes *Gambling-Like Risk-Taking in Large Language Models*, and the diagnostic vocabulary goes with it. That is a subtraction of claims and needs no new data. The substantive half of gbSA's concern, whether autonomy induces the behaviour, proved testable, and the answer changes what the paper claims: being given a choice is not what carries the risk, revisiting the choice every round is. The camera-ready states the mechanism that narrowly.

**The distortion analysis.** We agree with a3Zu that it was never validated against human judgement, and it still is not. What we could test is whether the result depends on our lexicon, and it does not. A second instrument, its categories taken from the meta-analysis the paper already cites and its expressions deduced from those published definitions without consulting our corpus, then frozen before any statistic, reproduces the contrast in every model. Human annotation is registered for the camera-ready; we do not claim it now.

**The neural decoding.** Accepted: the fitted readout is a monitoring signal, not a demonstrated cause, and the submission itself says so. Rather than reinterpret the null we ran the intervention that would settle it, on a separately defined direction, with a dose ladder, a removal arm, random-direction controls and a validity gate. That direction moves behaviour; the submitted readout does not, under steering or removal. It does carry information beyond the visible game, which is what a monitoring signal is, and we keep the two apart.

**The two easier fixes.** The matched-cap test now covers five models, at four caps for the API models and cap \$70 for the open-weight pair. Every headline difference now carries a 95% interval on the difference itself rather than two marginals, and we report that both pre-registered panel rules were negative, so what we claim is a controlled dissociation, not a panel-wide effect.

**What we have not done, and do not claim.** No human control group played this game, no human coder checked the flagged text, and we offer no mechanistic account of the readout. Where new data cut against our earlier wording we say so ourselves: the balance/round direction also steers on LLaMA, two of the four convergent constructs do not move consistently, and part of what the fixed arm buys is stopping early rather than betting better.
